About the project and where to learn more

Who built this and why, how to run it yourself, and trusted organizations for people affected by brain tumors.

About NeuroScan

NeuroScan began as a student project to see how well transfer learning can sort brain MRI slices into four categories. It grew into a full write-up because the interesting part was not the accuracy number but understanding what the number means.

It is open about its limits on purpose. A medical AI that hides its errors is worse than none, so the results page shows every mistake and the evaluation flaw we found along the way.

Built with

  • TensorFlow and Keras for training
  • VGG16 and MobileNetV2 as pretrained backbones
  • Flask for the local prediction server
  • Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript for this site

Run it yourself

Open the project folder, then start the local prediction server:

webapp/run.bat

To recompute the test metrics shown on the results page:

python src/evaluate.py

To rebuild this website from its source pages:

python website/src/build.py

Trusted places for information and support

Independent organizations that explain brain tumors, treatment and living with them.

  • National Cancer Institute: brain and spinal cord tumors

    Patient and clinician information from the US government cancer agency.

  • American Brain Tumor Association

    Education, support groups and a helpline for patients and families.

  • National Brain Tumor Society

    Patient resources, research news and advocacy.

  • World Health Organization

    Publishes the international classification used to grade nervous system tumors.

  • American Cancer Society

    Plain-language guides to diagnosis, treatment and support.

External sites are independent of this project, and their content can change.

How to cite

If you use ideas from this project, please cite the dataset it was trained on:

Jamil and Khan (2025). Multi-Class Brain Tumor MRI Dataset. Mendeley Data, doi:10.17632/82mtzd8x72.2. CC BY 4.0.

Important

NeuroScan is a student research project. It is not a medical device and must not be used to diagnose, treat or rule out any condition. Any MRI finding needs review by a qualified radiologist or physician. If you have symptoms or concerns, contact a doctor.
