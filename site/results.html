Where this model slips

Overall accuracy hides which mistakes are made. Reading the matrix:

  • Pituitary macroadenoma is the easy class. It sits in a distinctive place at the skull base, so the model finds almost all of them.
  • Meningioma is the hardest. It has the lowest precision and recall, and is mixed up with glioma and healthy slices.
  • Healthy slices are often called meningioma. That kind of false alarm is the safer direction for a screening aid, but it would still send people for needless follow-up.

The newer model, and the 100% that was not real

While training a second model we noticed validation accuracy of 100%. We then tested on the held-out images.

ModelValidation accuracy during trainingAccuracy on held-out test set
VGG16 (live demo)not reported80.4%
MobileNetV2 (newer)100%86.9%

The gap is a warning sign. Each scan produces many neighbouring slices that look almost identical. When slices are split into training and validation at random, near-copies of the same slice land on both sides, and the model is scored on images it has effectively already seen. The dataset files contain no patient identifiers, so a split by patient is not possible with the data we have.

Per-class recall for the newer model on the test set: pituitary 98%, glioma 93%, healthy 81%, meningioma 75%. Averaging predictions over a flipped copy of each image did not help (86.0%).

What we plan to do next

  1. Validate on a clean split

    Hold back part of the test data or use cross-validation, so checkpoints are chosen by a score that cannot be inflated by near-duplicates.

  2. Try a stronger backbone

    EfficientNet or ConvNeXt, with more layers fine-tuned, aimed at meningioma recall.

  3. Weight the hard class

    Class weights or focal loss so mistakes on meningioma cost more during training.

  4. Ensemble

    Average several models trained on different folds, and connect the best one to the live demo.

What these numbers mean for you

Even at its best this is a research prototype tested on one public dataset. It has not been validated on scans from other hospitals or scanners, which is the test that matters for real-world use.

NeuroScan

A student research project on classifying brain MRI slices. It is not a medical device and cannot diagnose, treat or rule out any condition. Anyone worried about symptoms or a scan should talk to a doctor.
