How it works

2

The image is resized to 128 x 128, converted to RGB and scaled to the 0-1 range, exactly as in training.

3

Classify

VGG16 features feed a small dense head with softmax output across the four classes.

What it recognizes

Glioma

Tumors that arise from glial cells in the brain or spinal cord. Often infiltrative with irregular borders.

Meningioma

Usually slow-growing tumors of the meninges, the membranes around the brain. Typically well-defined and attached to the dura.

Pituitary Macroadenoma

Tumors of the pituitary gland larger than 10 mm, located at the base of the brain near the sella turcica.

Healthy

MRI slices with no visible tumor, used so the model also learns what normal anatomy looks like.

Model and data

Architecture

  • VGG16 base pretrained on ImageNet, input 128 x 128 x 3
  • Last three base layers unfrozen for fine-tuning
  • Flatten, Dropout 0.3, Dense 128 (ReLU), Dropout 0.2
  • Softmax output over 4 classes
  • Adam, learning rate 1e-4, early stopping on validation loss

Dataset

  • Multi-Class Brain Tumor MRI Dataset, Mendeley Data (CC BY 4.0)
  • 1,137 images: 908 training, 229 testing, stratified 80/20
  • 15% of training held out for validation
  • Augmentation: brightness, contrast, horizontal flip, rotation up to 15 degrees
  • Evaluated with classification report, confusion matrix and per-class ROC/AUC

Important

NeuroScan is a student research project. It is not a medical device and must not be used to diagnose, treat or rule out any condition. Any MRI finding needs review by a qualified radiologist or physician.
